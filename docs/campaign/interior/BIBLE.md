# The Interior: the story bible

Written 2026-10-05 from the owner's three texts (PLAN.md section 1: the
in game mission script is the authority, then the cinematic treatment,
then the campaign document) and his answers (PLAN.md section 9). This
file is the world, the factions, the voices, the sensor language, the
music and the motifs. MISSIONS.md is the five missions, INTROS.md their
films, TECH-NEEDS.md what the engine must grow, OVERVIEW.md the one page.

## 0. The rules nothing in the story breaks

1. **Real land, invented everything on it** (PLAN 1, answer C). The
   land is recognisably northern Paraguay: red earth roads, pasture and
   soy, tree lines, cañadas, a slow brown river, dense forest. Every
   town, estancia, road, river, person, group and symbol is invented.
   No real town, department, person, armed group, emblem, incident,
   victim or coordinates appears in any line, string, texture, HUD or
   file name. The mocks' latitude, longitude, "AREA: CORDILLERA" and
   their serpent banner are not used.
2. **The country is never named on air.** The title says Paraguayan
   Combat Drone Simulator; inside the story it is "the Interior", "the
   region", "the north". No flag, no ministry, no real unit.
3. **People from the air, never in the room** (answer B). People on the
   ground are seen through the aircraft's camera, small, often half
   hidden. The operations room is shown only through its monitors, maps
   and stills, at most a hand. No face is ever on screen.
4. **No harm shown** (answer A). A strike on a person is allowed only on
   a HOSTILE CONFIRMED contact or an authorized target, and is shown as a
   sensor white out and the words "Threat stopped". No body, no gore, no
   slow motion, no kill banner, no cheering line. A strike on a civilian
   or a friendly is a consequence in the mission, never a reward.
5. **Unknown means unknown** (the script's global rule 1). Nobody becomes
   hostile for carrying an object, leaving a road, looking armed or being
   near a suspicious place. Classification moves only on evidence.
6. **No real operational instruction.** Tracking, strikes, interception,
   relays and interference are game mechanics, never procedures.
7. **No signal simulation** (owner, 4 October: "story moments only"). The
   relay and the M5 interference are scripted effects.
8. **Ustedes.** Every Spanish line that addresses the players addresses
   them in the plural. Characters speaking to each other use usted (a
   working military room is formal), which also keeps the checker's tú
   rule clean.
9. **Spelled numbers are allowed; digits never.** Act 1 forbids spoken
   quantities; the Interior keeps the owner's spelled numbers ("Twelve",
   "Fourteen", "forty-eight hours") because they carry its story: the
   whole conflict is fourteen people. The voice checker still refuses any
   digit.
10. **No em or en dashes** anywhere (CLAUDE.md). The script's cut off
    line "Major..." is written with a full stop or an ellipsis.

## 1. Premise

For more than a decade a small armed movement called **The Column** has
lived somewhere between insurgency, criminal myth, political memory and
rural folklore. It never held territory and never had conventional
strength, yet it survived. Operations came and went, leaders vanished,
cells broke up, and most of the country came to treat it as a relic.

Then the drones arrived, and for the first time somebody watched long
enough to see that the old conflict never ended. It changed.

The players are a new unmanned aviation detachment sent north under a
deliberately dull title, **Aerial Survey Support Unit**, to a vast rural
region known only as **the Interior**. Officially: infrastructure
imagery. Unofficially: months of small, strange reports that nobody has
put together.

The player never sees the whole war. Only fragments: roads, heat,
motorcycles, people vanishing under trees, vehicles stopping where they
should not, a symbol nobody has painted in years.

**Central principle:** the drone sees more than the pilot understands.
**Central fantasy:** "I am the person who can see what everyone else
cannot." **Tagline:** Everyone is looking for the enemy. You're looking
for the truth.

## 2. The world: the Interior

### 2.1 The land

About 16 by 16 km of northern Paraguayan landscape from open elevation
and land cover data (TECH-NEEDS N1): agricultural frontier and ranch
land in the south and west, a slow river winding through it, tree lines
along drainage, pasture with cattle, isolated houses with red tin roofs,
then dense forest to the north and east, broken by clearings. Hot, hazy
light; late afternoon gold; dust off the roads. It should look empty
from the road and full from the air ("Most of the Interior is not empty.
It merely looks empty from the road").

### 2.2 The invented places

searched with "Paraguay" and no town, district or locality of that name
was found there: Río Sereno (a town of that name exists in Panama, none

La Ceniza, Rincón Quemado, Puente Doble, Claro Viejo, Claro Nuevo, Pista
Cero, Monte Cerrado (only the cerrado biome came up), Loma del Vigía,
Cruce Tranquera, Corral Viejo, La Pista Larga, Vado del Manso, Rancho

## 3. The Column

Never shown as an army. No bases, no formations, no parade. Small
groups, concealment, dispersal, observation, avoidance (the script's
Column AI). The player should often be able to count them.

**The Old Column.** Older members, long memory, strong personal ties,
primitive but effective organisation, suspicious of outsiders. Its aim
has become survival, and at the end, keeping its own memory away from
what its young have joined. Look: patched tarps, old motorcycles,
improvised everything, the mark painted by hand and faded.

**The New Column.** Younger, less interested in the old ideology, more
willing to use technology, more aggressive, more willing to work with
criminal networks. Look: newer motorcycles, consumer electronics,
better batteries, disciplined spacing, modern clothing. It uses the old
mark and the old routes. "Same mythology. Different organization."

The relationship between the two is ambiguous on purpose until M5:
couriers pass between them; they never live together.

## 4. The Network

Introduced in M4. Not ideological. Vehicles, commercial infrastructure,
technology, money. It does not care what the Column believes; it cares
that the Column knows the terrain. The campaign's turn: the old conflict
is being absorbed into something larger. Look: clean, well kept vehicles,
consistent equipment, disciplined spacing, encrypted looking radios, no
symbol at all. Behaviour: confidence, coordinated movement, deliberate
logistics, counter surveillance (the script's Network AI). It is never
named as any real organisation and never carries real contraband on
screen: crates, documents, fuel, equipment.

## 5. The voices

Four voices carry the campaign, each with a job, so a line's speaker
tells the player what kind of information it is. Each is also a **guide**
for certain roles (MISSIONS.md 1.5): a guide's role lines are heard only
by the pilots in that role; story lines are heard by everyone. Voices are
generated by the war's pipeline (`tools/voice`), cloned from a synthetic
reference, so no real person's voice is used. The Kokoro stock voices
named are candidates to audition, checked against the installed Kokoro
list before building; Act 1 already uses `am_onyx`, `bf_emma`,
`bm_george`, `am_michael`, `am_puck` in English, so the Interior's
English voices are drawn from the others. Kokoro's Spanish set is small
(`ef_dora`, `em_alex`, `em_santa`), so two Spanish voices may share a
stock voice told apart by the reference clip and the radio colour,
decided by ear.

### VEGA: Major Tomás Vega, operations commander

- **Role:** command authority, restraint, final calls. Early fifties.
  Has watched institutions declare victories that solved nothing; does
  not trust dramatic conclusions. Starts seeing the player as one more
  sensor operator; by M5 trusts the player's judgement more than his own
  screen.
- **Guide:** nobody's in particular: Vega's lines are the command calls
  **everyone hears** (orders, rules, clearances, the final decision).
- **Manner:** few words, measured, never raises his voice; silence as a
  tool. Corrects language: what is known, said as known.
- **Sample lines:** "Observation first." "Unknown means unknown." "I
  don't need a theory. I need to know what you can actually see." "Do
  not turn uncertainty into certainty because someone is impatient."
  "Then call it one object consistent with a rifle."
- **EN direction:** low, dry, steady; short pauses before decisions;
  never dramatic. Candidates: `am_adam`, `am_fenrir`.
- **ES direction:** neutral Paraguayan formal, the same economy: "Observen
  primero." "Desconocido quiere decir desconocido." To the players
  always ustedes: "Si ven algo inusual, márquenlo." Candidate: `em_alex`
  with a lower reference.
- **Radio colour:** the room's console, clean, close.

### IBARRA: Sofía Ibarra, intelligence analyst

- **Role:** interpretation, uncertainty, historical context. Mid
  thirties. Has spent years on old photographs, confiscated documents,
  maps and biographies of the Column. Believes history explains it, then
  learns that the organisation in her archive no longer exists. The
  campaign's intellectual centre; the first to refuse an easy reading.
- **Guide:** ISR, TRACKER and RECON (finding, following, identifying,
  capturing).
- **Manner:** precise, quick, hedged when unsure ("consistent with",
  "possible"), never guesses twice; quiet when something matters (the
  symbol, the archive). Dry back and forth with Rojas.
- **Sample lines:** "That matches the pattern. It does not confirm the
  identity." "History is useful until people stop behaving like
  history." "The symbol is old. The people aren't." "Keep watching."
- **EN direction:** clear, mid pitch, thoughtful; drops almost to a
  whisper at the archive ("Oh."). Candidates: `af_heart`, `af_bella`.
- **ES direction:** educated, precise; "Mantengan la observación." "Eso
  coincide con el patrón. No confirma la identidad." Candidate:
  `ef_dora`.
- **Radio colour:** the analyst's desk: clean with a faint data hiss.

### ROJAS: Captain Adrián Rojas, ground liaison

- **Role:** ground urgency, human consequences. Early forties. Speaks
  for the units under the drones; cannot zoom out, rewind or orbit, and
  it shows. Frustrated by caution at first; by M5 the strongest advocate
  of persistent observation ("Nobody moves until the pilot confirms
  it.").
- **Guide:** GROUND SUPPORT and STRIKE (what the ground needs, what a
  strike costs).
- **Manner:** practical, direct, a little impatient, warm humour with an
  edge ("You're going to love this place.").
- **Sample lines:** "That's easy to say from fifteen kilometers away." "I
  don't need perfect. I need useful." "If you see something moving toward
  my people, tell me."
- **EN direction:** rougher, faster, a field radio voice; often on the
  move. Candidates: `am_eric`, `bm_lewis`.
- **ES direction:** colloquial Paraguayan, never vulgar; "Si ven algo que
  se mueve hacia mi gente, avísenme." Candidate: `em_santa`.
- **Radio colour:** a handheld or a vehicle set: engine under it, wind.

### FERRER: Lucía Ferrer, signals and systems officer

- **Role:** systems, drones, interference, dry humour. Late twenties.
  Heard briefly in M1, joins in M2, central from M3 as other aircraft and
  interference enter the story. Technically gifted, irreverent, less
  invested in the old conflict: the voice of the conflict becoming
  technological.
- **Guide:** RELAY and INTERCEPTOR, and the platforms' state for everyone
  (telemetry, feeds, the M5 disruption).
- **Manner:** dry, never comic; fast when focused. "Congratulations. The
  nineteenth century has discovered batteries."
- **Sample lines:** "That aircraft isn't ours." "I don't care what they
  call themselves. Somebody just put another drone in our airspace."
  "Video is degrading. Telemetry is still good."
- **EN direction:** bright, quick, deadpan. Candidates: `af_sarah`,
  `af_nova`.
- **ES direction:** young, crisp: "Están perdiendo el video de avanzada."
  Candidate: `ef_dora` with a younger, faster reference, told from
  Ibarra by colour.
- **Radio colour:** the systems bench: clean with a soft fan, a blip
  before her alerts.

### One off voices

- **ARCHIVE** (opening film): two or three old radio voices, recorded
  "years earlier", heavy band limit, hiss, overlapping. Lines: MISSIONS
  and INTROS. Generated, never sampled from any real recording.
- **COMMANDER** (M5_11, END_C): an external ground commander outside the
  cast, flat and procedural.
- **NEWS** (END_A): a reader under a room's noise, heard from a speaker.

### The players, on air

The radio says "pilot" (singular in the script's command lines, which
in a squad means whoever holds the role being addressed) and "Survey
One" for the ISR. A line never names a player. In Spanish, "piloto" in
the third person or ustedes; never tú.

## 6. The sensor and HUD language

Per the mocks (art direction only) and TECH-NEEDS N7, N14.

- **Picture modes:** EO, IR (white hot, black hot), low light, fusion; a
  row reading `EO  IR  MAP  TGT  LRF`. An IR inset in the corner.
- **Readouts:** ALT, SPD, HDG, local time, GIMBAL (depression and
  bearing), ZOOM (1x to 8x and more), a compass tape, a minimap with a
  scale bar. Never real coordinates, never a real area name: the
  readout reads `AREA: THE INTERIOR` or the sector ("SECTOR ALPHA").
- **Reticle and boxes:** a centre cross; a box only around a contact the
  room has discovered; a dimmed box while it is lost, a last known
  position circle after; search areas as soft circles; bearing hints as
  ticks on the compass tape. No glowing markers before discovery, no
  omniscient minimap.
- **Callouts** beside a box: `ID: UNKNOWN`, `ID: PERSON OF INTEREST`,
  never a fake percentage ("avoid fake numerical precision", the script).
  Confidence shown as the box's line weight: thin, medium, solid.
- **Classification states** (the HUD's words, the same in every mission):

  | State | ES | Meaning | Who sets it |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | UNKNOWN | DESCONOCIDO | seen, nothing known | discovery |
  | CIVILIAN | CIVIL | behaviour shows ordinary life | scripted evidence |
  | FRIENDLY | PROPIO | one of ours | scripted evidence |
  | PERSON OF INTEREST | PERSONA DE INTERÉS | behaviour worth following | scripted evidence |
  | COLUMN-LINKED | VINCULADO A LA COLUMNA | tied to the Column, unconfirmed | scripted evidence |
  | NETWORK-LINKED | VINCULADO A LA RED | tied to the Network | scripted evidence (M4_04) |
  | HOSTILE CONFIRMED | HOSTIL CONFIRMADO | confirmed; the only state a strike may target | scripted evidence, or a pilot's CONFIRM IDENTIFICATION (MISSIONS.md 1.6) |

  Group labels: `UNKNOWN THIRD PARTY`, `NEW COLUMN (UNCONFIRMED)`,
  `OLD COLUMN`, `NEW COLUMN`, `NETWORK`, `HOSTILE SURVEILLANCE` (an
  air contact).
- **Cards** (the script's common UI language): `PRIMARY OBJECTIVE
  UPDATED`, `SECONDARY OBJECTIVE`, `OPTIONAL`, `CONTACT CLASSIFICATION
  UPDATED / UNKNOWN > CIVILIAN`, `INTELLIGENCE ADDED`, `INTELLIGENCE
  CAPTURED`, `INTELLIGENCE UPDATED`, `MISSION RULE / ENGAGEMENT NOT
  AUTHORIZED`, `SEARCH AREA ADDED`, `LAST KNOWN POSITION`, `ARCHIVE MATCH
  SEARCHING...`, `TARGET LOST / REACQUIRE`, `INTELLIGENCE PICTURE N %
  COMPLETE`, `FINAL DECISION`, `ARCHIVE TRANSFER COMPLETE`. All string
  keys, en and es.
- **Strike presentation:** white out for about a second, the picture
  returns, the words `THREAT STOPPED`, nothing else.

## 7. Escalation: what the player learns

| Mission | The player learns | Roads mean | Antennas mean | The sky |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| M1 The Old War | I can see | scenery | camp equipment | the player owns it |
| M2 Eyes in the Forest | I can follow | the early warning system | the warning network | the player owns it |
| M3 No Man's Land | I must identify | movement and identification | communications infrastructure | something else enters it |
| M4 The Other War | I cannot watch everything | a logistics network | the Network's sophistication | contested |
| M5 The Last Column | seeing the truth does not tell me what to do with it | escape and fragmentation | the electronic contest | information is a battlefield |

Pacing: M1 almost too quiet; M2 investigative; M3 tense; M4 complicated;
M5 overwhelming, then abrupt quiet at the archive. The player begins as
a camera operator and ends as the person whose reading of the picture
decides what the mission meant.

## 8. Music

The score avoids heroic military music. Silence is a cue of its own:
whenever a realisation lands, reduce the music rather than raise it.

- **THE INTERIOR:** sparse, wide, atmospheric; low percussion; wind like
  textures; enormous distance. The bed of M1 and of every survey.
- **THE COLUMN:** old acoustic or analogue textures (a nylon string
  guitar, a harp's low strings, an old organ) processed electronically:
  history carried into the present. First under the camp in M1, faint.
- **THE NETWORK:** clean, cold, synthetic, rhythmic. Enters in M4 at the
  meeting, so the soundtrack says a different kind of organisation has
  arrived.
- **M5:** the three overlap, then drop out entirely at the archive.

Audio direction by mission (the script): M1 environment and sparse
radio; M2 close motor, wind, insects, distant vehicles; M3 rising radio
traffic; M4 layered radio and competing feeds; M5 overload, then quiet.

## 9. The Column's mark

An original mark, designed for the game, recognisable at 8x zoom on a
faded tarp:

- **Shape:** a row of five short vertical strokes, stepping down in
  height from left to right, standing under one long horizontal bar,
  all inside a circle left open at the bottom (a gap about a fifth of the
  ring). It reads as a small file of people walking under a roof of
  trees: a column under canopy.
- **How it is painted:** by hand with a brush, ochre or faded red on
  olive or grey canvas; strokes uneven; the old ones weathered half away.
  In the archive (M5) it appears newer and sharper on old cloth and
  paper: the "much newer in the archive" of the treatment.
- **What it is not:** no animal (no serpent), no star, no weapon, no
  tool, no flag, no letters, no colours of any real flag, nothing
  resembling a real group's emblem. Checked against the owner's eye
  before it is painted on anything.

## 10. Recurring motifs

| Motif | Its arc |
| --- | --- |
| **Roads** | scenery (M1) to warning system (M2) to identification (M3) to logistics (M4) to escape (M5) |
| **Motorcycles** | meaningless at first; then the old network's mobility; the player learns individual bikes (M5: "Same bike from the eastern observer.") |
| **Antennas** | camp equipment to warning network to infrastructure to sophistication to electronic contest |
| **Drones** | the player owns the sky (M1, M2); something else enters (M3); contested (M4); information becomes a battlefield (M5); the stinger: someone has been watching the watcher |
| **The mark** | a faded tarp (M1) to a "partial angle" callback (M2) to the archive's insignia (M5) |
| **Places that return** | Claro Viejo (M1 camp, M2 empty, M5 near the gathering), Ruta Vieja, Puente Doble and the colonia (M1 survey, M5 final flyover) |
| **Small things** | radio, motorcycle, solar panel, antenna, fuel can, notebook, old symbol, battery, tyre tracks |

Dialogue rule (the treatment): characters never explain what the player
can already clearly see. The player sees first, a character reacts
second, the objective updates third (the script's rule 2).
