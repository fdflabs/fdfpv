# Trick callouts: every aerobatic figure, named and judged

Written 2026-10-09, before the build, over `origin/main` at 729411fa. The
owner, 2026-10-09: "for the batalla de trucos, i need some arcade style
notices of when i complete any one of the available in the world rc or
airplane aerobatic moves...and then a score for each and every single
one...think those skateboarding games where you ride around through the map
and when you land tricks it tells you what the trick is and how well it was
executed".

## What exists already (read before this build)

- **Quads.** `src/game/trickdetect.js` names quad freestyle tricks from body
  rate integrals and laps round obstacle lines, 63 of the scoring workbook's
  90 rows (the rest need things the detector does not see: perches,
  disarms, wall rides on a named face, a building dive). Execution is one of
  five words from contact and dead time (CLEAN, SLOPPY, BUMP, MISSED,
  CRASH). `src/game/score.js` prices each trick from the workbook
  (`tricks.js`, generated by `tricks:gen`), applies execution, repeats, back
  to back halving and the obstacle factors, and chains tricks into a combo
  with a multiplier that banks after a 3 s gap or bails on a crash.
  `src/ui/scorehud.js` already draws that combo the skate game way. All of
  it is pinned by `trick:golden`, `tricks:golden`, `score:golden`.
- **Fixed wings** get nothing of their own: the same quad detector runs on
  them in freestyle, so a loop in the Extra scores as a quad "Flip" or
  nothing at all, and no aerobatic figure has a name.
- **Trick Battle** (docs/JAM-PLAN.md, #760, #772): the runner's own client
  scores its run with that scorer and sends `{ total, tricks, unique,
  crashes, last }` to the room; `last` is `{ name, execution, points }` and
  the room checks `execution` is one of the five words.

## What the player sees

1. Flying a fixed wing in Free Flight (freestyle maps) or in a Trick Battle
   run, every aerobatic figure in the catalogue below is named on the HUD
   the moment it is finished: the figure's name big, the grade word
   (PERFECT, SHARP, CLEAN, SLOPPY, ZERO; es PERFECTO, FINO, LIMPIO, SUCIO,
   CERO), the grade out of 10 and the points, and the running chain with its
   multiplier. Quads keep their freestyle tricks, with the same callout.
2. In Free Flight the callouts follow a setting, on by default.
3. In Trick Battle the watchers see the runner's callouts as they land.

## Detection (src/game/figuredetect.js)

Three layers over the plant's own state, every physics step:

1. **Samples, every 10 ms of simulation.** Nose, up and left wing from the
   quaternion, the flight path from the velocity, angle of attack from the
   two, body rates, and integrals over every 1 ms step of roll, pitch, yaw,
   world yaw and rotation about the flight path. Each sample gets a token:
   spin, pitchover, snap, tailslide, hover, yawover (the hammerhead's
   pivot), elevator, harrier, knife, roll, arc or line.
2. **Primitives.** A token held 40 ms opens a primitive; each carries what
   a judge measures: rotation per axis, path swept and its radius spread,
   mean line angle, height and heading in and out, roll rate spread, the
   pauses inside a roll (point rolls), how the path curves against the
   wings (inside, outside, knife edge).
3. **Figures.** A table of step sequences, longest first, names the longest
   run of primitives it can when the aircraft has flown level for 0.7 s
   (a climbing or diving line is a figure's middle, so only 3 s of it ends
   one) or ten primitives are waiting. Chains are figures named one after
   another; the scorer's combo is the chain.

Deterministic: decided on physics steps only, never frames; only +, -, *,
/ and sqrt (IEEE exact); angles compared as cosines against literal tables,
never Math.acos (CLAUDE.md). The same recording gives the same figures
(`figures:selftest`, `figures:plant`). Runs on whatever drives the plant:
solo Free Flight and the runner's own client in a Trick Battle (the room
never re-runs detection, docs/JAM-PLAN.md's trust boundary stands).

## Judging (the execution grade)

Every figure starts at 10 and loses points, in halves, the way the judges'
guides say:

- **Lines and angles**: 1 point per 15 deg off (FAI Sporting Code Section 4,
  Volume F3 Radio Control Aerobatics, Annex 5B, the F3A judges' guide; the
  same rule as the owner's brief). IMAC's Basic flying guide takes half a
  point per 5 deg and CIVA (Sporting Code Section 6) a point per 5 deg, both
  stricter than F3A; the game takes F3A's because it is the RC pattern
  standard and a pilot reads the aircraft from the ground. Applied to:
  vertical, 45 and level lines; entry and exit heading; bank at worst in
  a held attitude; the nose off vertical in a hover.
- **Rolls**: a constant rate (IMAC takes 1 point per change of roll rate;
  here 1 point per 15 % spread of the rate), stopped on the attitude
  (1 point per 15 deg short or over), the line and heading held through it.
- **Loops**: constant radius (1 point per 10 % spread), one plane (1 per
  15 deg of plane tilt), and a whole loop out at its entry height (1 per
  tenth of its diameter) and heading.
- **Spins, snaps, tumbles**: the stop, to the nearest quarter turn (1 point
  per 15 deg), and the heading kept through a snap.
- **Waterfall**: the stop as a tumble's, the plane of the pivot (1 point
  per 15 deg of tilt) and a point per 2 m the pivot moved, the hover's
  scale ("Ideally this is performed with little altitude gain or forward
  motion", Wikipedia, "3D Aerobatics").
- **Point rolls**: each point at its mark, 1 point per 15 deg off it
  (BAeA judging, "Slow Rolls": "each hesitation must be at the correct
  angle of roll").
- **Stall turns**: each vertical line judged whole, by its worst piece off
  the vertical and the farthest the nose was yawed off it (BAeA judging,
  "Stall Turns": "no yaw or wing-low"), 1 point per 15 deg each; the pivot
  is a yaw that turns the nose round.
- **Rolling circles**: one roll (Aresti 2.4.2.1; a wrong count is a hard
  zero), and 1 point per 15 deg of it left over or short at the finish
  (BAeA judging, "Rolling Circles": "Stop on heading and without any
  remaining bank angle").
- **Hover**: the nose on vertical (1 per 15 deg at worst) and a point per
  2 m of drift.

Grade words: 9.5 and up PERFECT, 8 SHARP, 6 CLEAN, above 0 SLOPPY, 0 ZERO.

## Points

- A figure's points: **K x grade x 2** (`POINTS_PER_K`). 2 puts a perfect
  Aresti loop (K 10) at 200, a quad Powerloop's workbook price, so quads
  and planes meet on one scale in a Trick Battle room.
- Everything else is the freestyle scorer as it is: repeats pay less
  (`repeatTrickFactor`), the same figure twice in a row halves, a chain
  multiplies while figures keep landing within the combo window and bails
  on a crash. Figures enter `score.land()` with their grade, which stands in
  for the workbook's execution factor; a trick without a grade scores
  exactly as before (the goldens prove it).
- **No proximity bonus** in this build. A bonus for flying low rewards the
  hills of one spawn over another and the room cannot check height; it
  needs the owner's yes (question below).

## Storage, sync, the room

- Nothing stored. The run's total already reaches the board and the room.
- The room (edge/rooms/jam.js) accepts `last.execution` in the five words
  only; a figure is sent with its execution word (CLEAN at 6 and over,
  SLOPPY above 0, MISSED at 0), so the room needs no change to accept it.
  The watchers' callout wants the grade and the figure id too: optional
  `last.grade` (0 to 10 in halves) and `last.figure` (an id from the
  catalogue), checked by the room and ignored when absent, which **needs a
  VM deploy** (PR 3).

## Builds on

score.js (land, combo, bank, bail), tricks.js (pricing by name),
scorehud.js (the skate model overlay), the audio system's existing cues,
roomjam.js / jam.js (the runner's `last`), main.js `feedRecogniser` (the
per step feed).

## What it does NOT do

No change to the quad detector or the workbook prices. No server side
detection. No proximity bonus. No new physics: figures that wait on the 3D
lane are detected on synthetic paths only. No ranking of figures against a
sequence card (that is a later competition mode, not callouts).

### Round 8 (2026-10-13, w38-hover-yaw): the yaw in the hover

Round 7 left the hover's rudder loop short (35 deg and 2.3 dB on the
Extra, 47 deg and 5.3 dB on the CAP) and asked what held the yaw tilt
stiff from 1 to 4 rad/s. Each yaw moment term was instrumented in a
scratch module and taken against the rudder's tilt, the Extra hanging
still at its hover stick (stiffness, N m/rad, positive restoring; at 1 /
2 / 4 rad/s):

| Term | 1 rad/s | 2 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| the fin in the wash, against the drift (fin_v) | -6.91 | -1.36 | +0.31 |
| the jet's normal force at the disc | +1.52 | +0.30 | -0.06 |
| the swirl at the fin | 0.000 | -0.001 | 0.000 |
| P factor, the gyroscope, the rigid body | under 0.13 each | | |
| the inertia (the sum, I w^2) | 0.14 | 0.59 | 2.41 |

- **Not a spring: a negative one, through the drift.** The tilt tilts
  the thrust, the hover drifts, and the fin in the wash met the whole
  drift as its crossflow and turned the nose into it. Its moment against
  the tilt was 2.3 to 49 times the inertia's from 2 down to 1 rad/s,
  which kept the tilt per stick flat at 0.55 to 0.70. The swirl has no
  stiffness: it is in the body's axes, the same at any yaw angle, and
  the jet turns with the body, as it should.
- **The cause.** The jet normal force (Selig, AIAA 2010-7938, eqs. 13 to
  16) is the momentum drag of the crossflow the disc turns onto the
  axis, so the air reaching the tail keeps only 1 - N_j / (mdot V_T) of
  it, k_j (0.80) of it gone hanging still. The plant spent that momentum
  twice: at the disc and again on the fin and the stabiliser. Now the
  surfaces in the wash meet what the disc leaves (plant_wing.c, the
  slipstream's crossflows; docs/FLIGHTMODEL.md). A momentum balance on
  Selig's own equations, not a step his paper states.
- **After.** The rudder's tilt per stick falls as a damped integrator:
  6.4 at 1 rad/s, 2.2 at 2, 0.92 at 4, 102 to 126 deg behind. Below about
  1.5 rad/s the tilt hangs on the hover's own swing (the drift the tilt
  starts, which the jet's force at the disc turns back); on the CAP's
  rudder that swing is lightly damped near 1.4 rad/s.
- **Measured (2026-10-13, PM / GM at the design), every loop meets:**

Detector and judge changes from the sweeps, each a correction to reality,
none a loosening (the synthetic suite passes 107 of 107):

- **Snap token on angle of attack**: a snap was any fast roll with the nose
  15 deg off the path. A real aileron roll on the Extra reaches that with
  sideslip alone, and every roll was cut into "snap" pieces. A snap is now
  the wing at 15 deg angle of attack (the flow under the wing, -d.u) while
  rolling at 4 rad/s.
- **Loop radius over the arc proper**: the spread is taken where the path
  turns at 0.4 rad/s or more; the corners into and out of the arc had
  radii of hundreds of metres and marked every half loop down 5 points.
- **A point is a stop**: a pause counts as a point only if the roll rate
  fell under 0.35 rad/s for 0.1 s. A barrel roll's sagging rate was read
  as a 2-point roll.
- **A knife edge is the wing in the vertical plane** (2026-10-11): the
  judge took the span within 26 deg of vertical (|l_z| > 0.9), which
  counts the sideslip that holds a knife edge up as a bank error. The
  Extra's E12 knife edge at 15 m/s holds 33 deg and the CAP's C12 at
  20 m/s 30 deg, so neither was named. The rule is the wing's (FAI
  F3P-AA 23: "During the knife edge the wing must be in the vertical
  plane"): the wing's normal against the path's horizontal normal, the
  same 26 deg tolerance (no source for it beyond the old choice), and the
  pass's grade off the same measure. `npm run figures:knife` flies E12's
  and C12's knife edges and must name both.
- **Each point at its mark** (2026-10-12, round 1): BAeA judging, "Slow
  Rolls": "each hesitation must be at the correct angle of roll", a point
  per 15 deg off for each point. A 2-point roll paused at 105 deg is now
  graded 5 under one paused on its back.
- **A stall turn's lines whole** (round 2): BAeA judging, "Stall Turns":
  below 8 m/s a rudder correction on the line is yaw samples and the slow
  path weaves past the arc threshold, so the line came in pieces and the
  first correction was taken for the pivot. A vertical line is now the
  run of its pieces, graded by its worst and the farthest it yawed; the
  pivot is a yaw that turns the nose round. A tightening.
- **A rolling circle's rolls** (round 2): BAeA judging, "Rolling
  Circles": "If the number of rolls is wrong - too many or too few - then
